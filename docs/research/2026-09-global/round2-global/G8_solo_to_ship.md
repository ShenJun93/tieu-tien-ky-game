# G8 — From Solo Idea to Shipped Game: What Separated Shippers from Demo-Loopers

Research date: 2026-09-26. Scope: global web research only, no project repo was read. Audience: a Vietnam-based solo developer with AI coding agents, building a Unity xianxia mobile action game, who is stuck making demos.

**Verification legend.** `[V]` means the fact was confirmed on a fetched page during this session. `[S]` means it came from a search-engine summary of the listed page, which is weaker. `[U]` means it is background knowledge I could not verify this session because the web-search budget ran out part way through. Treat `[U]` items as leads to check, not as facts.

## 2. Patterns among shippers

**1. The first paid version was small, and people saw it early.** 20MTD took about 2 months. Brotato had a demo after about 3 months. Vampire Survivors reached EA in about a year, and Soul Knight launched in about a year. Stardew is the exception: 4.5 years. Even so, Barone went public on Greenlight after about one year, and a publisher took on ports and localization `[V]`. Shipping fast did not mean shipping bad. Each of these games had **one tight loop, polished enough to be fun, and little else.**

**2. Art was a production decision, not the milestone.** Vampire Survivors used bought packs `[V]`. Hollow Knight chose hand-drawn 2D to avoid 3D complexity `[V]`. Kairosoft reuses one style across 60+ games `[V]`. Grimvalor shipped with a single artist/animator `[V]`. Mega Crit keeps rough placeholder art in EA on purpose, because otherwise "people would judge the game as if it's a 1.0" (Casey Yano, GamesRadar+). **Readable, consistent and cheap** beat bespoke and beautiful.

**3. They cut the prototype down to its core.** Soul Knight dropped floor-painting: "最开始是用枪涂地板，后来发现不太给力" ("started with guns painting floors; it wasn't great", GCORES). Dead Cells dropped its tower-defense preparation phase `[V]`. Neon Abyss tested its random systems on paper before writing code `[V]`.

**4. Outside players were the validation, not internal gates.** Balatro had 300+ beta testers and a Discord a year before launch. LocalThunk wrote that "demo and beta players knew more about how to play the game…" than he did (localthunk.com). 20MTD used r/playmygame and festivals. Its demo median playtime was 1h15, a signal that could have been read before launch `[V]`. Slay the Spire balanced from player metrics `[V]`.

**5. Practice at finishing came first.** Bippinbits made 14 itch.io games before Dome Keeper: "We needed 10 game jam games to get good enough" (Game World Observer). Brotato was Blobfish's third commercial game `[S]`. Kairosoft grew out of a hobbyist's freeware `[V]`.

**6. Early access and live updates were part of the plan.** Hades built EA in from day one and gave each update a headline feature `[S]`. Slay the Spire shipped weekly updates for 14 months `[S]`. On mobile, the equivalent is closed testing, then a soft launch, then global release.

**7. Publishers and platforms arrived after the proof, not before.** Korean publishers now scout through demos and EA and do not fund from zero (Sisa Journal-e, 2026). Raw Fury found Dome Keeper through #screenshotsaturday `[V]`. Playstack found Balatro on Steam `[V]`.

**8. The finishing canon** (paraphrased; these pages were not fetched this session, so read them first-hand):
- **Derek Yu, "Finishing a Game":** don't start over, don't roll your own tech, do the parts you avoid, cut features, and finish `[U]`.
- **Jonas Tyroller:** make many small prototypes quickly, then commit to the one with the strongest signal `[U]`.
- **Thomas Brush:** market from day one and treat wishlists as the gate `[U]`.
- **Pirate Software (Thor):** cut scope hard and ship something small `[U]`.

## 3. Patterns among people who get stuck, and how some escaped

| Trap | Evidence | Escape that worked |
|---|---|---|
| Years on one big project with no traction | flanne: "I really was tired of working on Spiritlink Tactics for over 5 years" (HowToMarketAGame) | A **2-month side project** with a hard ship date became 20MTD |
| Scope grows once money or enthusiasm arrives | Hollow Knight grew from about 2 hours to a huge world, "we just kept going and going" `[S]`. Soul Knight Prequel took 4 years with 3 people `[V]` | Lock scope before funding. Treat post-launch updates as the place for growth (Hades, StS) |
| Restarting or rewriting | Cave Story restarted and took about 4 years `[V]`. Hades rewrote its engine, but with 20 staff `[V]` | A solo dev cannot afford to rewrite. **Freeze the tech** once the loop is fun |
| Polishing one slice forever, with no outside players | Common thread across the counter-examples. Neon Abyss found that tester leniency hid problems `[V]` | Put outside players in within weeks: itch.io, r/playmygame, TapTap, a Discord beta |
| Building tools and process instead of the game | Consistent with Derek Yu's "don't roll your own tech" `[U]` | Use engine and store defaults. Build a tool only if it saves hours on **content** this week |
| Solo isolation and burnout | A Korean solo dev spent ₩60M over 2 years and reported loneliness and health costs (DCInside) | A co-working space, a public devlog, and a peer or co-op tester group |

**What applies to this developer:** building governance and tooling is the same trap as "rolling your own tech". It produces the feeling of progress with zero player-facing evidence. The cure, supported by the cases above, is **external players on a fixed calendar**.

## 4. Production planning for a solo commercial mobile action roguelite

**Realistic timeline.** A solo developer with AI coding agents and bought or AI-assisted art should aim for **9–12 months to a soft launch**, and 15–18 months to a global launch. The case benchmarks: Soul Knight took about 1 year with 5–10 people. Vampire Survivors took about 1 year solo. Dome Keeper took 17 months with 2 people. Grimvalor had 4 people. A xianxia 3D action game with bespoke art pushes this toward Soul Knight Prequel's 4 years, so the art and camera scope must be fixed early.

**Google Play mechanics are hard dates.** A new personal developer account needs a **closed test with at least 12 testers, opted in continuously for 14 days**, before it can apply for production. That review "usually takes seven days or less". **Pre-registration stays locked until production access is granted** (Google Play Console Help, `[V]`). Plan roughly **4 weeks** from "closed test starts" to "can open pre-registration".

**Minimum launch content (my recommendation, sized from the cases above, not sourced).**
- 1 playable hero with 3 distinct build archetypes. Add 2 more heroes after launch.
- 3 chapters (biomes) × 6–10 stages, plus 1 endless or ascension mode.
- 15–20 enemy types, including 4–6 elites.
- 3 chapter bosses plus 1 final boss.
- 40–60 skills, techniques or upgrades, with 8–12 synergy families. Dead Cells had about 50 weapons at 1.0 `[V]`.
- A meta-progression layer: cultivation realm, gear, talents.
- 6–10 hours to first completion and 20+ hours of replay.
- Tutorial, settings, save and cloud save, localization for EN/VI plus ZH or ID, and a crash-free build on low-end Android.

**Soft-launch KPIs.** Use the current GameAnalytics or Sensor Tower genre benchmarks. I did not verify any figures this session. The common heuristic is D1 retention of about 35–40% and D7 of about 10–15% for mid-core action.

**Keeping progress visible every week.** Each week produces one thing a player can perceive:
- a 15–30 second clip,
- a changelog, and
- one new or improved content unit (an enemy, a skill, a stage or a boss).

Post it every Friday. Content counts are the progress metric. Internal gate documents are not.

## 5. The anti-demo-loop playbook

**Rules**
1. **One build, always shippable.** The main branch ships an Android APK every week. There is no "slice", only "the game, version N".
2. **External players every 2 weeks from week 4 onward.** A closed test on Play, plus a Discord or Zalo/Facebook group, plus r/playmygame and TapTap. The test is what people do, not whether a gate passes.
3. **Freeze the tech and process at week 6.** No new frameworks, hooks, governance or tools unless a tool saves time on content that same week. Governance time is capped at 1 hour per week.
4. **Buy or generate the art. Don't invent it.** Choose one art bible and one asset family (bought packs, AI-assisted and cleaned up), stay consistent, and cap each asset by the time it takes. Upgrades come after soft launch. This is the Vampire Survivors and Kairosoft approach.
5. **The 70% rule.** When a feature is fun and readable, move to the next content unit. Polish is batched into two planned polish months.
6. **A cut list, not a wishlist.** Anything not in the launch-content spec goes to "post-launch updates". That list is the live-ops roadmap, as with Hades and StS.
7. **If the loop isn't fun by week 8, try the 20MTD test.** Build a 6–8 week "small game" variant, such as a survivor-like xianxia spin-off, and ship it. A shipped small game beats a perfect slice. This also follows the Bippinbits practice-to-finish lesson.

**Commitment devices**
- **A public devlog every week**: YouTube Shorts or TikTok plus Facebook, in Vietnamese and English, and a Discord.
- **A Google Play closed-test date announced publicly** in month 3. The 12-tester, 14-day requirement then becomes a forcing function.
- **A pre-registration page live by month 6**, after production access.
- **An event deadline**: a Steam Next Fest or itch.io demo, a Vietnam indie showcase, or a Korean or SEA expo submission date. The Korean solo dev above used PlayX4 and BIC.
- **A soft-launch date in a small market** such as the Philippines, Vietnam or Malaysia, told to testers and followers.

## 6. Twelve-month roadmap skeleton to soft launch

| Month | Deliverable (always an installable APK) | Public milestone |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Core loop for one hero (move, attack, dodge, 3 skills, 5 enemies, 1 stage), using bought or placeholder art locked to an art bible | Devlog #1 and a Discord |
| 2 | Run structure: stage, rewards, level-up choices, death and restart, 15 upgrades | First outside playtest with 10+ people |
| 3 | Chapter 1 complete: 8 stages, 1 boss, 8 enemies. Basic meta-progression | **Google Play closed test starts** (12+ testers for 14 days) |
| 4 | Retention layer: cultivation realms, gear, saves. Balancing from test data | Apply for production access. Short trailer |
| 5 | Chapter 2, 2 build archetypes, 30 upgrades | Open closed test (100+ players) |
| 6 | **Polish month 1**: game feel, VFX readability, audio, haptics, UI | **Pre-registration live** |
| 7 | Chapter 3, final boss, 45+ upgrades, endless mode | Content-reveal devlogs |
| 8 | Monetization: premium unlock or light IAP plus rewarded ads, analytics, crash reporting | Monetization test with testers |
| 9 | Low-end device performance, localization for EN/VI plus one more language, store assets | Store page and screenshots final |
| 10 | **Polish month 2**: onboarding, first-session funnel, accessibility | Press and creator outreach in VN and SEA |
| 11 | Content freeze. Bug-fix only. Release candidate | Soft launch date announced |
| 12 | **Soft launch** in 1–3 small markets. Instrumented KPIs | Soft launch live, then iterate toward global |
